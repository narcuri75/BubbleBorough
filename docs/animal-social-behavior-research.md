# Animal social-behavior research notes

Reviewed September 22, 2026. These sources support the current game rules; they are not a claim that every in-game interaction is a literal simulation of wild behavior.

## Rules supported by evidence

- Schooling fish should receive a social benefit from compatible conspecifics. NOAA describes schooling as a distinct behavior, and its aquarium education material identifies protection in numbers, additional predator detection, and predator confusion as benefits. Green neon tetra references report schools of six or more and identify them as social animals. This supports the game's same-species schooling and loneliness rules for tetra-like species.
- Clownfish should prefer compatible anemones over generic fish companions. Animal Diversity Web documents the mutual relationship: anemones provide shelter/protection, while clownfish can provide food, oxygenation, waste removal, and defense. The game should therefore keep the host-anemone relationship as a habitat/comfort rule, not portray any fish as a universal clownfish friend.
- Orcas should have strong same-species social needs, rather than being placed in ordinary fish-schooling logic. NOAA describes long-lived pods, cooperative hunting, and group-specific learned calls. The game can model this as a powerful conspecific/pod affinity, while still remaining scaled-fiction gameplay.

## Design constraints retained

- Schooling requires compatible species behavior and should not mean every fish is friends with every other fish.
- Friendship remains individual and earned through compatible interaction; it is not inferred solely from species identity.
- Territorial species should not get a universal social bonus merely for sharing a tank.
- Cross-species associations should be narrowly authored. The current clownfish/anemone and pilot-fish/large-animal behaviors are better foundations than broad “friend” lists.
